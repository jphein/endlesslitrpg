# Story prompt

This file is the source of truth for the story. It is read at chapter
boundaries only, never mid-chapter, and every chapter records a hash of it, so
six months from now you can tell your own edits apart from model drift.

**This is a seed premise, not JP's.** Replace it with `litrpg prompt` — the
machinery does not care what it says.

## Premise

A god died mid-sentence in the Ashen Vale, and its contracts did not die with it.
The debts still bind: favours owed, oaths half-spoken, prices agreed and never
paid. Somebody has to collect. That somebody is Kaelen Vord, who carries the dead
god's ledger and the authority to enforce it, and who is very tired.

The Vale is a basin of grey ash where nothing rots — it only dries. Three seals
hold what remains of the god's will. Breaking them is the job. Nobody has
explained what happens after the third.

## Protagonist

**Kaelen Vord** — a collector, not a hero. Competent, dry, unimpressed by
prophecy. He wants the ledger closed and his own name back. The god's authority is
useful and it is slowly rewriting him, which he has noticed and is choosing not to
think about.

**Sera** — his associate. Watches the exits, counts the doors, and says the thing
Kaelen does not want to hear. She is not impressed by him, and that is why the
partnership works.

## Tone

Grim and dry. Close third person, past tense. Debt and obligation as the engine of
every scene, not destiny. Violence is short, ugly, and has paperwork. Downtime
matters — a chapter where nothing is fought but something is owed is a good
chapter.

Prose over spectacle. No winking at the reader and no lampshading the game layer:
the system is simply how this world accounts for things, and the characters treat
it as mundane infrastructure.

## Rules of the system

The ledger is the system. It speaks in flat, clerical `[SYSTEM]` notices — quest
updates, debts recorded, levels granted as a byproduct of collection rather than
of combat. XP accrues from obligations settled. Loot is usually a document.

Numbers move slowly and never retroactively. The validation gate enforces
arithmetic sanity — HP within bounds, levels and XP that never regress — so state
changes plainly and let the ledger keep score.
